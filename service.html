For service companies

Forty brands on one truck. Ninety seconds to find the page.

You have the manuals. That was never the hard part. The hard part is that the unit in front of your technician is one of forty brands you service, the fault code means something different on each, and the customer is standing there. Answerlore answers from your own manuals, cites the page, and says so plainly when your documentation doesn't settle it.

The shortage is the technicians, not the people

Your industry says it plainly: lots of people can change switches and bulbs, far fewer really understand commercial equipment.1 When the ones who do are booked out, every “quick question” from the field interrupts a real repair. The senior technician everyone calls is also the one whose day is most expensive to interrupt.

First-time fix

The number that runs a service agency is whether the truck comes back. A second visit costs the drive, the hour, the part that should have been on board, and the customer's patience. Most callbacks are not a skill problem: the answer existed, in a manual, on a page nobody could reach in the ninety seconds available.

The retiring senior technician

The deepest knowledge in your company is not in any manual: the sounds, the smells, the “that model always does this in the third year.” It lives with people who are retiring. Answerlore's gap log is how that knowledge stops being a person and starts being a document: every question your technicians ask that nothing answers is queued with a running count and an owner. Your senior technician answers each once, in writing, permanently.

Your technicians are already asking an AI

They are. On their phones, in the parking lot, between calls, because it is faster than calling the office. What they get back is fluent and unverifiable, and it has already been tested in this trade and found convincing but often wrong.2 The choice is not whether your technicians use AI. Multi-brand by design

One knowledge base across every line you service. Each answer cites the specific manufacturer's manual, revision and page, so brand-specific procedures never blur together. Service bulletins, known-defect notes and warranty adjudication rules sit in the same place as the manuals, which is the material that is genuinely hard to get.

For dealers

The spec question shouldn't cost a senior tech an hour.

You sell and service equipment you didn't build. Your inside sales desk fields compatibility, parts and warranty questions across every line you carry, and the person who actually knows is one senior tech who's stretched thin between service calls. Dealers name this problem the same way everywhere: tribal knowledge, walking out the door.

The math your trade press already did

A technician down for 30 days costs a dealership roughly $44,570 in service revenue, by the industry's own calculator.1 79.3% of ag equipment dealers rank technician availability as their top concern, which means the replacement for your retiring expert probably isn't coming.1 The knowledge has to stop living in one person.

What changes with a support brain

Your inside sales and service coordinators answer spec, compatibility, parts and warranty questions from the manuals, with the page cited, instead of walking to the shop to interrupt whoever's senior.

New hires are useful in week one: they ask Answerlore what they'd be embarrassed to ask a person for the third time.

Your senior tech's remaining years become a documentation engine: the gap log queues the most-asked undocumented questions, he answers each once, and it's written down for good.

Multi-OEM by design

One knowledge base across every line you carry. Each answer still cites the specific manufacturer's manual and page, so brand-specific procedures never blur together.
